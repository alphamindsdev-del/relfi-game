# **REL■FI FULL PLAYER MANUAL** 

###### **Complete AROPE Fiction Detection & Friction Guide** 

### **1. Core Principle** 

Rel■Fi is not about judging truth or falsehood. It trains players to detect reasoning patterns and 

psychological structures inside statements. Players identify how ideas influence belief systems, 

authority logic, social structures, and evidence claims. 

### **2. The AROPE Framework** 

All detectable reasoning patterns in **_Rel■Fi_** fall into five fiction types: Authority Dependence, 

Rigidity, Oversimplification, Power Imbalance, and Evidence Strain. 

### **3. F1 — Authority Dependence** 

**Definition** : Obedience or belief based on recognized hierarchy or legitimacy. Mirror Questions: • Is 

obedience expected because someone holds a recognized authority role? • Is the idea accepted 

because it comes from a sacred or legitimate source? Common Contexts: • Parenting • Education • 

Government and law • Religious leadership • Workplace supervision Examples: • A parent sets 

rules a child must follow. • A judge directs courtroom proceedings. • A religious leader's instruction 

is followed without debate. • A manager assigns mandatory tasks to staff. Typical Frictions: • Duty 

vs independence • Pressure to obey • Trust in leadership • Responsibility weight 

### **4. F2 — Rigidity** 

**Definition** : Ideas presented as fixed, absolute, and not open to questioning. Mirror Questions: • Is 

doubt discouraged? • Are there words like 'always', 'never', or 'must'? • Are alternative views treated as rebellion? Common Contexts: • Strict doctrines • Extreme moral codes • Ideological absolutism • 

Cultural taboos Examples: • 'This rule must never be questioned.' • 'Good people always behave this way.' • 'There is only one correct belief.' • 'Anyone who disagrees is wrong.' Typical Frictions: • 

Fear of questioning • Intellectual suppression • Conformity pressure • Anxiety about dissent 

### **5. F3 — Oversimplification** 

**Definition** : Complex realities reduced to neat, single■cause explanations. Mirror Questions: • Is a complicated issue reduced to one cause? • Are human nuances ignored? • Does it feel too tidy for real life? Common Contexts: • Moral judgments • Social issues • Cultural stereotypes • Motivational slogans Examples: • 'Poor people are poor because they are lazy.' • 'Success comes only from hard work.' • 'Bad behavior is caused by bad character.' • 'If you believe strongly, everything will work out.' Typical Frictions: • Misunderstanding of reality • Blame shifting • Shallow reasoning • False certainty. 

### **6. F4 — Power Imbalance** 

**Definition** : Unequal control between people who are natural or social equals. Mirror Questions: • 

Are these people social equals? • Is inequality happening where fairness should exist? • Is one 

dominating another without justified hierarchy? Common Contexts: • Gender inequality • Racial 

discrimination • Peer favoritism • Relationship domination • Social exclusion Examples: • A qualified woman is denied promotion while male peers advance. • Two classmates commit the same offense but only one is punished. • One romantic partner controls shared finances unfairly. • Citizens of the same country are treated differently due to ethnicity. Typical Frictions: • Resentment • Suppressed voice • Unequal opportunity • Emotional or social restriction 

### **7. F5 — Evidence Strain** 

**Definition** : Claims presented as factual without adequate support or verification. Mirror Questions: • Is this presented as proven without evidence? • Does it use scientific language without proof? • Are extraordinary claims made without verification? Common Contexts: • Pseudoscience • Rumors • Conspiracy claims • Misleading statistics Examples: • 'Studies prove this herb cures all diseases.' • 'Everyone knows this is scientifically proven.' • 'This technology reads your thoughts.' • 'History clearly shows this happened' (without sources). Typical Frictions: • False confidence • Misplaced trust • Manipulation through certainty • Spread of misinformation 

### **8. Quick Decision Guide** 

**Step 1** — Does the statement rely on a recognized authority or hierarchy? If YES → Authority 

Dependence 

**Step 2 —** Are people who should be equals treated unequally? If YES → Power 

Imbalance 

**Step 3** — Is questioning discouraged with absolute language? If YES → Rigidity 

**Step 4** — Is a complex issue reduced to a single simple cause? If YES → Oversimplification 

**Step 5** — Is something presented as proven without solid evidence? If YES → Evidence Strain 

### **9. Understanding Friction** 

Friction is the psychological tension created by these reasoning patterns. Players learn to detect emotional, social, intellectual, or moral tensions that influence behavior. 

###### **Types of Friction: •** 

Emotional tension • Social restriction • Suppressed autonomy • Moral pressure • Fear of dissent •False certainty 

### **10. Final Insight** 

**_Rel■Fi_** sharpens perception. Players learn to see how ideas shape thinking styles, power 

structures, and belief systems. Mastering **AROPE** improves critical reasoning, social awareness, and strategic discussion skills. 

#### **11. Facts Today — Constructive Reframing Mode** 

**_Rel-Fi_** does not stop at detecting weak reasoning. After identifying Fiction Patterns and the Friction they create, players can shift into Facts Today — a constructive thinking mode that restores balance, fairness, and clarity. Facts Today helps players replace distorted reasoning with healthier, modern perspectives without attacking beliefs or traditions. Instead of only asking: “What’s wrong with this reasoning?” Players also ask: “How can this idea be viewed more fairly and thoughtfully today?”

### **12. Official Mapping Rules** 

**_Rel-Fi_** encourages thoughtful and fair identification of reasoning patterns. 

Players should focus on the dominant structure of reasoning rather than personal beliefs, emotional reactions, or topic sensitivity. 

## **Primary Mapping Principle** 

Always identify the main reasoning pattern causing tension in a statement. 

###### **Ask** : 

_What kind of thinking structure creates the strongest friction here?_ 

Map based on structure — not on whether the topic is religious, political, cultural, or scientific. 

## **Pattern Priority Guide** 

When multiple fiction patterns appear, choose the most dominant one. 

##### **Use these priority checks:** 

###### **Authority Dependence** 

→ When belief relies mainly on hierarchy, titles, or sacred legitimacy. 

###### **Power Imbalance** 

→ When tension comes from unequal control, responsibility, or fairness. 

###### **Rigidity** 

→ When ideas are framed as absolute, unquestionable, or allowing no alternatives. 

###### **Oversimplification** 

→ When complex issues are reduced to single-cause explanations. 

###### **Evidence Strain — Fiction of Exclusion** 

Evidence Strain should be treated as the fiction of exclusion. 

Use Evidence Strain only when no other fiction pattern clearly fits and the central issue concerns unsupported claims or lack of verification. 

This rule prevents Evidence Strain from being overused, since many statements may contain some level of unverifiable elements. 

##### **Use Evidence Strain primarily when:** 

- A statement is presented as proven fact without support 

- Scientific or factual language is used without verification 

- Extraordinary claims lack credible evidence 

- The main tension concerns proof, evidence, or credibility 

If the main friction involves power, hierarchy, rigidity, or oversimplification, those patterns should be mapped first. 

## **Dominant vs Secondary Fiction** 

Some statements contain more than one fiction pattern. 

###### **Players may identify:** 

- ⭐ **Primary Fiction** — strongest reasoning flaw 

- ✨ **Secondary Fiction** — supporting pattern 

However, gameplay scoring prioritizes the Primary Fiction. 

## **Neutrality Principle** 

**_Rel-Fi_** does not judge beliefs, traditions, or communities. 

It analyzes reasoning structures only. 

Mapping should remain: 

- analytical 

- respectful 

- structure-focused 

- free from ridicule or hostility 

## **Goal of Mapping** 

The purpose of fiction mapping is to: 

- sharpen perception 

- improve reasoning awareness 

- encourage balanced discussions 

- prepare players to use Facts Today constructively 

Rel-Fi is about how ideas are framed, not attacking what people believe. 

### **13. Rel-Fi Gameplay System** 

Rel-Fi can be played in two main formats: 

###### • **Solo Mode** 

###### • **Seer & Skeptic Mode** 

The host will announce which mode is active before each round. 

Each round presents a statement. Players analyze the reasoning structure and map the dominant fiction pattern. 

Players earn tokens based on their decisions. 

### **14. Game Objective** 

The objective of Rel-Fi is to: 

- correctly map the dominant fiction pattern in a statement 

- understand how ideas influence belief systems and reasoning 

- balance persuasion and skepticism 

Players accumulate tokens during rounds. 

The player with the highest token count wins the game. 

### **15. Round Structure** 

Each round follows the same structure. 

##### **Step 1 — Statement Appears** 

A claim or idea appears on screen. 

##### **Step 2 — Analysis Period** 

Players analyze the reasoning pattern behind the statement. 

##### **Step 3 — Decision** 

Players select the fiction pattern they believe best maps the statement. 

##### **Step 4 — Host Reveal** 

The host reveals the correct mapping and explains the friction. 

##### **Step 5 — Tokens Awarded** 

Players receive tokens based on their choices. 

### **16. Solo Mode** 

Solo Mode tests independent reasoning. 

Only one player maps the statement. 

##### **Solo Mode Process** 

1. The statement appears. 

2. The player selects the fiction pattern. 

3. The host reveals the correct mapping. 

##### **Token Rewards** 

Correct mapping → 2 Tokens 

Incorrect mapping → 0 Tokens 

### **17. Seer & Skeptic Mode** 

Seer & Skeptic Mode introduces persuasion and doubt. 

One player becomes the Seer. 

Another becomes the Skeptic. 

The Seer receives hidden clues in the Secret Room. 

The Skeptic must decide whether to trust the Seer or act independently. 

### **18. The Secret Room** 

The Secret Room gives the Seer limited information. 

The Seer may receive: 

- a narrowed list of possible fiction patterns 

- partial clues 

- occasionally the exact mapping 

- sometimes no clue at all 

The Seer must return and pitch their answer to the Skeptic. 

### **19. The Seer** 

The Seer attempts to persuade the Skeptic. 

The Seer may: 

- explain reasoning 

- argue confidently 

- attempt to convince the Skeptic to follow their mapping 

The Seer’s goal is to gain influence. 

Even if the Seer is incorrect, persuasion may still earn tokens. 

### **20. The Skeptic** 

The Skeptic listens to the Seer’s pitch. 

The Skeptic must decide: 

- Follow the Seer 

- Call the Bluff 

- Go Solo 

The Skeptic’s goal is to identify the correct mapping while resisting persuasion when necessary. 

### **21. Token Award System** 

##### **Seer convinces Skeptic and mapping is correct** 

Seer → 2 Tokens 

Skeptic → 2 Tokens 

##### **Seer convinces Skeptic but mapping is incorrect** 

Seer → 1 Token 

Skeptic → 0 Tokens 

This reflects how persuasion can still earn influence even when wrong. 

##### **Skeptic rejects the Seer and chooses correctly** 

Seer → 0 Tokens 

Skeptic → 2 Tokens 

##### **Skeptic rejects the Seer but is incorrect** 

Seer → 0 Tokens 

Skeptic → 0 Tokens 

### **22. Multiplayer Seer Mode** 

Rel-Fi can also be played with multiple Seers. 

In this format: 

- one player becomes the Skeptic 

- all other players become Seers 

Each Seer attempts to convince the Skeptic that their mapping is correct. 

The Skeptic must decide which Seer to trust or whether to go solo. 

### **23. Role Rotation** 

To maintain fairness, the Skeptic role rotates each round. 

Example with four players: 

Round 1 → Player A is Skeptic 

Round 2 → Player B is Skeptic 

Round 3 → Player C is Skeptic 

Round 4 → Player D is Skeptic 

All other players act as Seers. 

### **24. Group Size Formats** 

##### **Two Players** 

- 1 Seer 

- 1 Skeptic 

Roles alternate each round. 

##### **Three to Six Players** 

- 1 Skeptic 

All others are Seers attempting persuasion. 

The Skeptic role rotates each round. 

### **25. Psychology of the Game** 

Rel-Fi reflects real-world belief dynamics. 

Ideas often spread through persuasion before verification. 

Some voices gain influence through confidence and narrative. 

Others challenge ideas through skepticism and critical thinking. 

The game explores the tension between: 

- persuasion 

- belief 

- skepticism 

- reasoning 

### **26. Winning the Game** 

After the final round, players count their tokens. 

The player with the highest number of tokens wins. 

Victory reflects a balance of: 

- strong reasoning 

- persuasive ability 

- intelligent skepticism 

### **27. Final Note** 

Rel-Fi is not about attacking beliefs. 

It is about understanding how ideas influence thinking. 

Players learn to detect reasoning patterns, understand persuasion, and engage in thoughtful analysis of claims. 

### **28. HOW TO SPEAK IN REL-FI** 

Rel-Fi is not just about choosing the right answer. 

It’s about how you think, explain, and influence others. 

In every round, you will either be a Seer or a Skeptic. 

Each role has a clear way to speak. 

- **AS THE SEER (How to Influence Clearly)** 

- 🔮 

As the Seer, you have access to a **_Secret Code._** Your role is to guide others toward the correct pattern—without revealing the clue directly. 

##### 🎯 **Use this simple structure every time:** 

##### **1. State the Answer (Be Clear and Confident)** 

Say the fiction pattern directly: 

### “The correct mapping is F5 — Evidence Strain.” 

Do not hesitate. Confidence increases influence. 

##### **2. Explain the Reason (Focus on the Pattern)** 

Explain why the statement fits that pattern: 

### “This statement makes a strong claim, but gives no clear 

### proof.” 

Keep it simple. Focus on reasoning, not emotion. 

##### **3. Guide the Decision (Influence Others)** 

End by directing the group: 

### “There’s no way to verify it… this is Evidence Strain. Lock it in.” 

You are not just explaining—you are leading a decision. 

##### ⚠ **Avoid This:** 

- “I think it might be…” 

- “Maybe it’s…” 

- Explaining too much 

- Revealing the clue directly 

##### 💡 **Tip:** 

A strong Seer is not the smartest person— 

it’s the one who communicates clearly and confidently. 

# **AS THE SKEPTIC (How to Think Independently)** 🧠 

As a Skeptic, your role is to **_test the Seer’s reasoning._** 

You are not here to oppose blindly—you are here to think clearly. 

##### 🎯 **Use this structure when you speak:** 

##### **1. Acknowledge (Optional but powerful)** 

### “I see your point…” 

This keeps the conversation calm and focused. 

##### **2. Challenge the Pattern** 

### “But this doesn’t look like Evidence Strain to me…” 

Be clear about what you disagree with. 

##### **3. Present Your Own Reasoning** 

“This feels more like **_Rigidity_** because it uses words like ‘must always’.” 

Support your answer with logic. 

##### 💡 **Tip:** 

Do not focus on the Seer. 

Focus on the structure of the statement. 

# **YOUR DECISION MOMENT** ⚖ 

In every round, you will: 

- 🔒 Lock your answer (your first thinking) 

- 🔁 Re-lock your answer (after hearing the Seer) 

This is where the game happens. 

Ask yourself: 

- “Do I trust my reasoning?” 

- “Did the Seer change my mind?” 

- “Am I following… or thinking independently?”

- Safer

##### ✔ **Resist the Seer** 

- Riskier 

- Higher reward 

Choose based on your confidence.
